# Channel blueprint: Practical AI, Proven

## Positioning

**A faceless English channel that tests practical AI decisions against real tasks, then explains the mechanism and tradeoffs clearly enough that viewers can repeat the decision.**

The channel does not exist to repeat AI news. Every video must do at least one of these:

- reproduce a useful result;
- compare two approaches on the same task;
- explain a mechanism that changes a practical decision;
- build a workflow viewers can inspect and adapt.

## Viewer promise

“Give me a few minutes and I will show what changed, prove the important part, explain why it works, and tell you when not to use it.”

## Default episode

1. **0–15 seconds — proof or useful tension:** one result, failure, constraint or contradiction.
2. **15–45 seconds — minimum context:** why the decision matters now and what will be tested.
3. **Three to five ideas:** each has a distinct visual purpose and sourced claim.
4. **Demonstration:** actual product UI, screen recording, output, code or structured comparison.
5. **Limits:** uncertainty, uncontrolled variables, cost, privacy and edge cases.
6. **Decision rule:** a practical next action, not motivation.

## Editorial moat

- Repeatable test briefs and saved artifacts.
- Primary-source claim ledger.
- Original diagrams that explain the tested mechanism.
- Honest negative and mixed outcomes.
- Decision rules that survive beyond the current news cycle.

## Originality boundary

Use evidence-first hooks, visual explanations and meaningful scene changes. Never reproduce reference scripts, graphics, title/thumbnail identity, intros, exact pacing, or creator voice. Reference assets stay in private research only.

## Success criteria per video

- The first sentence creates a specific reason to continue.
- The promise is understandable by 15 seconds.
- Every material claim has a source.
- At least one real result or mechanism is shown.
- Limitations are visible before the conclusion.
- The final recommendation follows from the evidence.
