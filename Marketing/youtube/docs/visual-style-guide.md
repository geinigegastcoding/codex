# Visual style guide

## Direction

Original clean neutral system: light gray base, white evidence surfaces, dark ink, indigo accent and green evidence color. It intentionally avoids the reference channels’ recognizable visual identities.

## Frame hierarchy

One primary message, one supporting visual, and at most two quiet accents. Keep important content at least 112 px from horizontal edges and 92 px from vertical edges at 1920×1080.

## Typography

- Hero: 104 px
- Section title: 78 px
- Supporting text: 44 px
- Readable labels: at least 28–32 px
- Captions: 46 px, maximum two lines

Do not shrink text to solve overcrowding. Split the idea across time.

## Scene grammar

- Kinetic text for a short claim or decision.
- Browser frame for product evidence.
- Screen recording for behavior over time.
- Code block when exact implementation matters.
- Diagram for causal flow.
- Comparison table for identical criteria.
- Source label for factual proof.

## Motion

Use frame-derived interpolation only. Opening meaningful changes roughly every 2–5 seconds; later changes roughly every 4–8 seconds; major section changes every 20–45 seconds. These are defaults, not quotas. Motion must clarify hierarchy, state change or causality.

## Captions

Phrase-level, maximum two lines, punctuation-aware grouping. Do not cover code or product controls. Source labels and captions occupy separate reserved zones. One-word flashing is off by default.

## Evidence rules

- Label mockups as mockups.
- Never fake a product output.
- Keep source labels readable but secondary.
- Reference-channel screenshots remain private research assets and never enter `public/` or published renders.
