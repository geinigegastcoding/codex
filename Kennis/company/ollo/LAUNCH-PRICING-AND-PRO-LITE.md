# Ollo launch pricing and Pro Lite policy

**Status:** launch recommendation, updated 19 October 2026. RevenueCat10.28.1 and a Test Store-only purchase preview are implemented; sandbox success, cancellation, failure, entitlement activation and restore were observed on Android16. No real Google Play offer/transaction is enabled. Ollo has no ad SDK or rewarded-ad flow. The prior app state had no billing or internet permission. This note proposes policy and dashboard setup; it does not implement rewarded ads. Existing pricing analysis remains in [[PRICING]].

## Recommendation

For a future commercial launch, test one paid offer: **Ollo Pro, a one-time lifetime purchase**, with **€14.99 (about €15) as the Netherlands price hypothesis**. During current product testing, keep every feature free. The proposed paywall is a non-gating preview: it can show the provisional Pro matrix and localized price concept, but it must not hide or disable functions, initiate a real paid purchase, or imply these Pro features already exist. Explicitly labeled Test Store simulations are available for integration checks and never gate the free app. Let Google Play localize the actual buyer price by Play country and show the localized store price in the app when purchases are enabled; English UI does not mean USD pricing. The €15 figure is a hypothesis, not a validated global price or a promise that every market will see that number. Do not add a subscription until Ollo has ongoing service value that warrants recurring billing.

Keep the current useful product free. The permanent purchase should fund future, optional power-user configuration and content, rather than charge for the blocker users rely on. Ollo does not yet have enough clearly distinct paid functionality to justify presenting Pro as an implemented feature bundle. Validate the named paid additions with users before launching a real paywall.

Treat **Pro Lite** as a later experiment, not a launch dependency. If explored, let a user affirmatively choose one rewarded ad to unlock one clearly scoped Pro Lite session. A fixed “one minute of ads” promise cannot be guaranteed: AdMob creatives have a variable reward countdown of 5–30 seconds, high-engagement ads can allow up to 60 seconds, and ad pods can show two videos back-to-back. State the actual reward before each ad; do not promise a particular ad duration or availability. A candidate reward is “one day of access to the listed Pro extras after a completed ad,” but only after real-device testing confirms the inventory and expiry behavior. Never make the reward necessary to use Ollo’s core or complete an interruption flow.

## Proposed feature boundary

| Free during testing and core-free at launch | Pro lifetime, provisional future extras | Pro Lite candidate after validation |
|---|---|---|
| Every currently implemented function remains accessible while testing. At commercial launch, keep selected-app blocking and app selection; essentials; manual and practice pauses; opening prompts, conscious access, grace period and optional quiz; schedules/routines; emergency access; protection status, permission recovery and diagnostics; current charts, history, conservative outcomes; current offline activities, favorites, export and local deletion; privacy explanation and offline license information free. | **Proposals only; no current feature is gated.** Possible future paid extras are optional configuration scale and customization, such as additional routine presets, more personal opening-plan presets, and richer offline learning customization/content. Each must be built, maintained, and named accurately before being sold. Preserve existing settings and saved data. | **Proposal only.** Could offer the same future nonessential Pro extras for a clearly stated short period. Identify exactly which extras are included. Never change protection, routine enforcement, or the user’s existing configuration when the temporary grant ends. |

Do not paywall or ad-gate selected-app protection, essentials, emergency access, manual break/recovery controls, active protection, schedules a person already configured, current charts/history, exports, deletion, privacy/recovery explanations, permission repair, or Android settings access. Never interrupt an active block, protected routine, setup, emergency path, or recovery flow with an upgrade prompt. Preserve saved configuration and history if a paid entitlement is unavailable or expires; remove only access to future use of clearly optional extras.

Do not retroactively move an ordinary current function behind Pro. All functions stay free throughout testing, including after showing the preview paywall. Future Pro should feel like extra value through distinctive optional features and customization, not a cut-down version of core Ollo. The most credible future Pro value is extra customization/content that Ollo can maintain. Widget, cloud sync, notification reminders, website filtering, and selective Shorts/Reels blocking are not implemented and must not be listed or sold as available features.

## Pro Lite policy and trade-offs

Rewarded ads are an opt-in, in-app exchange; the publisher is responsible for granting the promised reward. Google requires a conspicuous, accurate disclosure of both the action and reward before each instance. Users must be able to decline, and declining or dismissing must not impair normal app use. Do not imply Google endorses or verifies the reward. A suitable pattern, if built, is a calm “Watch an ad to try [named extra] for 24 hours” offer with equally clear “Continue without it.”

An ad SDK is a meaningful product/privacy change. Ollo now has internet permission for the authorized RevenueCat purchase integration; app usage, rules and app choices remain local, and no advertising SDK is installed. RevenueCat already introduces pseudonymous purchase-service traffic, including startup/customer-info refresh. Adding an advertising network would be an additional privacy and product change. AdMob introduces network requests, ad identifiers/diagnostics and third-party data flows; it requires updating the privacy and Play Data safety disclosures, the privacy page, manifest/permission review, and EEA/UK/Swiss consent handling. For personalized ads in those regions, Google requires a Google-certified TCF-integrated CMP; AdMob’s UMP is its app SDK option. The ad request must honor consent and child/age handling where applicable. Confirm supported audience and consent approach before enabling ads. (This is a platform-policy implementation checklist, not legal advice.)

Reward verification and expiry are awkward in Ollo’s current accountless/offline design. A client callback can be replayed or the local clock changed; robust AdMob server-side verification needs a server endpoint and conflicts with a strict local-only/no-server promise. Local expiry can be made resistant to ordinary clock edits with monotonic elapsed time while the device remains booted, but reboot, restore/backup, app-data clearing, offline completion and clock manipulation still need a documented behavior. Do not add a fake unlock or claim tamper-proof verification. If the no-account/no-server constraint remains, treat the reward as a best-effort local trial, limit abuse through simple local caps, and be candid about limitations; if robust fraud control is required, defer Pro Lite until the owner accepts the service and privacy change.

Ad monetization also creates variable revenue per user and a potentially poor fit with a deliberate pause product. A reward impression does not guarantee a fixed earning; fill and eCPM vary by country, consent, ad demand and user. Ads can add friction at the exact moment Ollo asks users to pause. Measure ad opt-in, completion, reward completion, refusal, user retention, consent-related no-fill and crash rate before deciding whether the extra complexity helps. If the privacy or UX cost exceeds the modest ad revenue, omit it.

## Price and lifetime economics

€14.99 is consistent with the owner’s approximately €15 direction and the existing €9.99/€14.99 research cells, but should be tested with a single offer description and localized Play prices. Prior price work uses 21% VAT for Netherlands examples and illustrates different Play fee cases; proceeds remain uncertain until Ollo confirms its Play fee enrollment, install classification, tax/fee basis, refunds and actual settlement reports. Do not advertise net proceeds or copy €14.99 to every country. Google Play country determines the transaction currency and Play Console supports country-specific price edits; select availability and inspect all target territories before release.

Lifetime is attractive because Ollo currently has no ongoing hosted service and users avoid an accumulating subscription. It also creates an indefinite maintenance obligation: users may reasonably expect the included extras to keep working across supported Android and Play changes. Keep scope bounded and promise “lifetime access to the Pro features listed at purchase,” not every future feature or service. Maintain the purchase entitlement across reinstalls/store-account restore. Separate restoring the purchase from restoring local settings/history; do not imply purchase restoration recovers local app data.

## RevenueCat / Play setup guidance for parent integration

Use one Google Play **one-time product**, configured in RevenueCat as a **non-consumable**, with product ID `lifetime`. Attach it to the existing entitlement `ollo_pro`. RevenueCat documents that a non-consumable tied to an entitlement unlocks it permanently; a consumable could be bought again. Keep one current Offering, e.g. `default`, with one **Lifetime** package containing that product; have the app read the current offering and localized product price instead of hardcoding a euro price. Create/activate the in-app product and country availability/price in Play Console first, then import/connect it in RevenueCat, attach entitlement, put it in the offering and verify the graph before purchase UI is enabled. No subscription base plan is needed.

Use RevenueCat’s **Android app-specific public SDK key** in production/release. A RevenueCat **Test Store key is for debug-only**: official docs warn that the SDK intentionally crashes in release builds if it detects the Test Store key and explicitly say never submit it to Google Play. Use Play license testers / Play sandbox with the Android platform key for release-track purchase testing. A public SDK key is not a secret, but it must match the intended RevenueCat app/store. Parent reports a test public key is supplied; keep it out of any release configuration.

RevenueCat Customer Center is optional and likely more than Ollo needs for lifetime-only at first. In its default form the main action is “Missing Purchase”/restore; cancellation and plan changes are subscription-oriented, and plan changes are unavailable for lifetime purchases. If chosen, explicitly configure the screen for restore/support and remove irrelevant subscription management choices. Android integration is supported in `purchases-ui` 8.12.2+ (the planned 10.28.1 exceeds this minimum) and requires a Customer Center dashboard configuration; promotional-offer setup is part of RevenueCat’s general Customer Center onboarding, but there is no lifetime cancellation offer to manage. A simple first-party “Restore purchase” action plus clear support route is sufficient for this one-time SKU. Use Customer Center only if its restore/support UX is worth the added UI package/configuration.

Entitlement expiry should not be used for Pro Lite unless the app models it as a separate temporary grant. Do not grant a temporary ad reward by attaching or revoking the lifetime `ollo_pro` entitlement; keep lifetime ownership permanent. A dedicated local `pro_lite` state should only enable the limited extras and should not be confused with a purchased entitlement.

## Competitor and related precedent

The [LockFeed iOS App Store listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/lockfeed-earn-screen-time/id6819274567) currently states five free quiz-based app unlocks, followed by one-time Lifetime Access for unlimited quiz unlocks and standalone practice. It says the regional price is shown before purchase and offers Restore Purchases. This is useful packaging precedent for letting users experience a finite learning loop before charging for unlimited learning, but it is iPhone-only and does not establish Android or global willingness to pay. The US page surfaces several price rows ($0.99, $2.99, $4.99, $9.99, $19.99, $30.00), likely reflecting offers/availability; they are not a single standard price and should not be compared as global WTP.

[one sec’s official FAQ](https://one-sec.app/faq/) publishes €3.99 monthly, €14.99 annually and €99.99 lifetime, while noting regional differences; it keeps one target app free and says it does not show ads. This is vendor-published euro evidence, not a verified Android Play checkout price. [AppBlock’s official Premium page/help](https://appblock.app/premium/) shows broader subscription/lifetime packaging but asks Android users to check in-app pricing. These examples show that one-time pricing exists in the category, not that users will pay Ollo €15.

## Open owner decisions before launch

1. Which exact optional extras will be shipped as Pro on day one? Do not release a paid item until this list, copy, and saved-data behavior are definite.
2. Should Pro Lite be dropped to preserve Ollo’s ad-free/offline promise, or explored later with an explicit consent/network/privacy change and best-effort local reward handling?
3. Which Play countries will be enabled at launch, and who will review the localized catalog prices and settlement reports?
4. What is the supported-device/maintenance scope behind “lifetime,” and what support route will lifetime buyers use?
