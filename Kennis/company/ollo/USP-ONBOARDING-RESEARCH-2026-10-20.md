# Ollo USP and onboarding evidence check

**Checked:** 20 October 2026. **Scope:** quick primary-source check for Android feature overlap and the claim that longer, useful onboarding improves retention. This is not a full competitor audit. Google Play descriptions are developer claims, not hands-on validation. No uniqueness, effectiveness, demand, or willingness-to-pay is established here.

## Findings

- **Exercise unlocks already exist on Android.** PushUp: Screen Time & Fitness and Push Up Time / Push & Post describe camera-counted push-ups, squats and other exercises in exchange for app access. Their listings describe Accessibility and camera use; one claims local camera processing and cloud backup. This confirms concept overlap, not the accuracy, uptake, safety or outcomes of either implementation. A push-up unlock is not an Ollo USP. [PushUp Play listing](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.ascent.pushup) · [Push & Post Play listing](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.deepdiveapps.pushupscounter)
- **Learning gates and local data also overlap.** Learn Before describes selected-app quiz unlocks, adjustable unlock duration, local app selections/progress, and a current demo before permission requests. QALock offers app-lock learning tasks, custom/imported flashcards, limits and local settings. These make Ollo's finite offline quiz and onboarding demo parity, not exclusivity. [Learn Before Play listing](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.sakkata.learnbefore) · [QALock Play listing](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.pi3141.qalock)
- **Purpose/finish and content filtering are busy spaces.** Stay Focused advertises app/site blocking, timers, keyword filtering, Shorts and Reels blocking. ShortBlocker says it selectively blocks short-form video while leaving messaging and other app functions accessible. The listings do not establish that Ollo's exact intention-to-finish flow exists in either product, but they undercut broad claims around content filtering or keeping useful parts of social apps available. [Stay Focused Play listing](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.stayfocused) · [ShortBlocker Play listing](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.snorltd.shortblocker)
- **Backup and widgets remain unverified here.** Push & Post claims cloud backup; that is some overlap with recovery, not proof of an accountless/offline settings restore. This source sample did not establish a competitor's widget parity. A widget would still be a convenience surface rather than a defensible USP. Wider market verification is needed before any “first/only” claim.
- **No primary evidence found that more onboarding screens or time cause better retention.** Six preregistered experiments and a large field experiment found that choice framing/sequence can increase app-feature adoption and onboarding completion; the paper studies uptake, not downstream retention or longer-vs-shorter flows. A randomized longitudinal ePRO study found an app platform had higher retention than paper, but retention was comparable to its website arm; the app group had higher survey adherence. It tested platform/usability and motivational elements in a clinical research context, not onboarding duration or consumer utility apps. These findings support making important setup choices clear and usable, not extending Ollo onboarding to improve retention. [Reeck et al., *Journal of Marketing* (2023)](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/00222429221141066) · [Börner et al., *Journal of Participatory Medicine* (2025)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11967695/)

**Practical read:** treat onboarding length as a cost; add a step only when it helps a user configure, understand, or safely recover a real first-use outcome. Compare current short onboarding with a specific optional, useful step; assess successful setup, permission comprehension, recovery, completion/drop-off, and later self-reported usefulness. Ollo has no telemetry, so a small consented walkthrough can test usability but cannot establish retention lift.

## Candidate value hypotheses for neutral discovery

| Hypothesis (not a decision) | Android overlap / uncertainty | Main implementation and privacy risks | Neutral tester question |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. A user-chosen physical or non-physical task can make an unlock feel earned. | Direct exercise-unlock listings exist; task confirmation is not novel. | Camera/pose model accuracy, sensitive sensor access, physical exclusion/injury, public-space awkwardness, false unlocks, and safeguards for essentials/emergencies. Manual confirmation is cheaper but trivially bypassed. | “How, if at all, would you want to confirm a task before opening an app?” |
| 2. A purpose paired with a self-chosen finish cue may help someone use an app for a task and leave. | Intention prompts and quick-return features have appeared in earlier Ollo research; exact Android flow parity remains uncertain. | More taps and nagging; typed purpose may expose personal details. Do not infer sent messages or task completion from another app. Keep text ephemeral/optional and allow done/continue/skip. | “Think of the last time you opened this app for a reason. What, if anything, would have told you that you were done?” |
| 3. A longer setup that reaches one useful, understood configuration may outperform a shorter setup that does not. | No evidence here that *length* improves retention; competitor onboarding/demo flows exist. | Drop-off, permission fatigue, rushed consent, and mistaken belief that Ollo is active before permissions are granted. | “What did you expect Ollo to do after setup, and what—if anything—was unclear or unnecessary?” |
| 4. Portable settings recovery without an account may matter to users who change phones or reinstall. | Cloud backup is claimed by one exercise-blocker listing; offline/accountless restore parity is not resolved. | Backup exposes app choices/schedules; malformed or old files can corrupt configuration; restored rules must not imply Android permissions are active. | “Have you ever had to set an app like this up again? What would you expect to carry over?” |
| 5. A home-screen widget could make protection state and one control easier to reach. | No competitor widget verified in this quick sample; Ollo already has a Quick Settings tile. | Stale status could falsely imply protection; widget state/update behavior differs by launcher. Must not bypass controls or show sensitive app names. | “When would you use this instead of opening Ollo or using the Quick Settings tile?” |
| 6. Selective blocking could preserve useful tasks inside apps while avoiding Reels/Shorts. | Strong listing overlap across Stay Focused and ShortBlocker; this area is not distinctive. | Accessibility access to screen structure/text, brittle app-version/language selectors, accidental blocking and platform-policy scrutiny. | “Which parts of an app do you still need when you want to avoid another part?” |

## Source limits

Eight sources were reviewed: six current Google Play listings, two original research papers. Listings reflect what developers claim on their pages; they do not prove feature quality, retention or policy compliance. The retention literature here is adjacent and does not answer whether Ollo should add onboarding steps. No user interviews, installs, device tests or broad market search were conducted.

## Parent product decisions - 20 October

These are product choices and hypotheses, not market validation or uniqueness claims.

1. **Lead with a complete purpose-to-finish journey.** The direction is: choose why you are opening, choose what done means, receive a useful check-in, then make the return to offline life easy. Current presets/timed cues are implemented; free text, chosen finish conditions and the richer return flow remain requested implementation work. Do not advertise those additions as shipped yet.
2. **Offer different ways to earn access.** Exercise, a chosen nonphysical task or learning should be optional choices that respect essentials, emergency access and fixed blocks. Explain what completion sensing actually measures; manual confirmation is self-report and proximity cycles are not verified exercise form. Exercise/task unlocking is still unimplemented at this checkpoint.
3. **Make personalization concrete.** The new setup saves a personal reclaim activity and real app/counting/pause choices. Android-derived totals name their source and uncertainty. Portable settings and the mascot widget make the plan easier to keep and reach. This coherent experience is the value hypothesis; individual features are not claimed unique.
4. **Keep the useful foundation free.** Normal blocking, essentials/emergency routes, basic figures and accountless settings portability remain free. Keep every feature free during testing. For later Pro, prioritize genuinely implemented cosmetic/mascot/theme choices and advanced customization of the requested challenge/quiz experience. Selective filtering could add paid value only after it works within a clearly tested scope. Do not sell planned capability.
5. **Validate usefulness before length or price.** Ask a tester to configure distracting apps, an essential, a personal reason and a counting preference, then explain what protection is actually enabled. Observe whether each question helps and whether they can finish setup with access declined. Later, test a real purpose/finish session and ask which optional extras they would pay for. The existing lifetime price proposal remains an unvalidated hypothesis. No analytics SDK or telemetry experiment is added by this decision.

Implementation order follows reliability and trustworthy setup, then purpose/finish, optional earn-access challenges, contextual alternatives/learning and the remaining requested feature groups. The full eleven-feature scope remains in [[PRODUCT-WORK-2026-10-20]].
