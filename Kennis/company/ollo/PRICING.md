# Ollo pricing and packaging hypothesis

**Status: hypothesis for validation; no billing or prices are implemented.** Prepared 16 October 2026 for Ollo Android in the Netherlands / euro area. Prices below are proposed test cells unless explicitly identified as competitor prices. They are not measured willingness to pay, a revenue forecast, or a claim of store or regulatory approval.

## Product and value

Ollo is a native Android, offline-first self-control tool. It has no account, ads, server, or internet permission. The value is a dependable, understandable pause between an automatic app opening and the user’s next choice, while keeping essential apps and Android recovery controls available. Its protection is voluntary and should never be marketed as impossible to bypass.

The best initial value metric is **one person’s ongoing access to Ollo’s deliberate-use tools on their Android device**. Do not price per blocked app, minute, routine, quiz answer, or measured “minutes saved”: these are arbitrary or unreliable measures of user value. No seat tiers are appropriate for this consumer app.

## Recommended first packaging

Keep Ollo’s core useful and safe at no charge: selected-app blocking, essentials, manual pauses, opening prompts, the optional three-question gate, routine basics, emergency access, honest statistics, export and deletion. These are the product’s trust foundation. A paywall should never appear during setup, an active block, a protected routine, an emergency path, or permission recovery.

If user research supports charging, test **one Ollo Plus entitlement**, unlocked by a single purchase, for optional advanced configuration and newly built, maintained tools. Candidate paid additions could include more schedule/routine presets and richer offline learning customization. Do not place the existing safety behavior, essential apps, Android settings access, or current core blocker behind it. Selective Shorts/Reels support is not implemented; do not sell or promise it until compatibility is built and verified for supported app versions.

Start with **one-time lifetime purchase only**, not monthly/yearly subscriptions. The current product is local, accountless, and has no ongoing hosted service; a recurring charge would need a clear continuing service value. A one-time purchase also fits people who reject another subscription. Revisit annual pricing only if Ollo later provides ongoing value that requires sustained work, such as a maintained, version-verified compatibility service. Do not offer lifetime alongside a subscription at launch: there is no evidence yet to justify the extra choice or to price the future maintenance obligation.

**Initial price cells to test, not launch decisions:** €9.99 and €14.99 one-time, paid once for the supported Ollo Plus features, with the entitlement restored through the purchasing Google Play account on supported Android installations, subject to Play’s current terms. This describes store-account purchase restoration; it does not restore Ollo’s local routines or history. Test the same product and explanation at each price. If a monthly comparison is needed in interviews, use €1.99/month as a clearly hypothetical alternative; do not implement it until there is recurring value. No free trial is needed for a permanent unlock; let users understand and use the free core before any optional upgrade prompt.

## Ethical purchase experience and offline behavior

- Offer an upgrade from a calm settings or feature-discovery surface after the user has experienced Ollo. State the exact paid features, price, payment type and restore path before purchase. Use no countdowns, guilt, streak loss, repeated nags, or blocked access to essential settings.
- Keep the currently active protection flow running if billing is unavailable, the purchase is pending, canceled, declined, or unverified. Never interrupt a protected countdown or suddenly relax an active block to force a purchase.
- On successful purchase, cache the entitlement locally and unlock paid features. Restore the permanent entitlement from the purchasing Google Play account when available; this restores the purchase right, not local routines/history. Check Google Play’s purchase state when connectivity returns; show a neutral “purchase status needs checking” message if offline. Do not require an Ollo account or internet to use the free core. A locally cached one-time purchase should not silently expire solely because the device is offline.
- If purchase verification fails, do not grant an unverified entitlement. Keep all free features and current protection available, provide retry and restore-purchase actions, and explain the issue without implying that the user’s protection failed. A user may continue using Ollo offline.
- If a future subscription is introduced, cache access through its known paid-through date and a documented short verification grace period. After that period, make paid-only creation/editing unavailable in a predictable way, while allowing the user to inspect and end existing paid routines safely. Never terminate an active protection flow or block system recovery because renewal failed. Confirm detailed expiry and grace behavior against Google Play Billing’s current requirements before implementation.
- Preserve data on uninstall/reinstall as far as Android permits. With no account and no cloud, explain that local configuration may be lost and that a purchase can be restored through the store; distinguish purchase restoration from restoring local data.

## Unit economics examples

These are arithmetic examples, not a forecast. They assume a Netherlands consumer-facing price **including 21% VAT**, with VAT removed as `gross / 1.21`; the fee is modeled on the VAT-inclusive gross amount. Google Play’s current EEA schedule has separate rows: the **first-$1M annual-earnings** cell spans all transaction columns and reads “10% + 5% billing fee”; enrollment in Google’s 15% service-fee tier is required. The standard row is 10% + 5% for auto-renewing subscriptions, 20% + 5% for other transactions from new installs, and 25% + 5% for other transactions from existing installs. The Play Games Level Up / Apps Experience row is 10% + 5%, 15% + 5%, and 20% + 5% respectively. Google defines new versus existing installs by whether first Play install/update occurred before or after the 30 June 2026 EEA rollout date. The source notes that the billing fee applies to purchases of in-app digital services/features using Play Billing. Confirm Ollo’s enrollment, install classification, fee basis and tax treatment in Play Console/accounting before launch.

\* `gross − modeled VAT − modeled fee`. The 15% column is only a first-$1M, enrolled-tier illustration; it is not assumed for Ollo. The 25% and 30% columns are standard EEA other-transaction new-install and existing-install sensitivities, combining the stated service and billing fees. All cases exclude refunds, chargebacks, income/corporate taxes, development, support and any tax/accounting differences. VAT and fee are separated to make assumptions visible; do not interpret the result as accounting advice or a guaranteed payout.

At these prices, a directly recurring €0.00 hosting bill is part of Ollo’s present architecture; it does not mean development and support cost nothing. RevenueCat’s [published pricing](https://www.revenuecat.com/pricing) and [billing documentation](https://www.revenuecat.com/docs/welcome/set-up-revenuecat/account-management) say the plan is free below US$2,500 MTR, then charges **1% of all MTR** in a billing period above the threshold, not merely 1% of the amount above $2,500. The docs give $2,600 MTR → $26. MTR includes one-time purchases and renewals before store commission and taxes. Thus, at $3,000 MTR the listed fee is $30 for that period, subject to RevenueCat’s plan and account terms; store fees are additional.

RevenueCat could simplify purchase status and cross-store entitlement handling, but it brings an external service and normally requires network access and purchase-event data leaving the device. That is a material change to Ollo’s current no-internet-permission/local-only posture. Compare direct Google Play Billing with RevenueCat before implementation; if RevenueCat is chosen, first design and disclose the added data flow and offline behavior. No CAC, conversion rate, retention, subscriber count or profit is assumed here.

## Global campaign and localized prices —18 October2026

The English/global campaign does not establish worldwide commercial availability or a USD offer. Keep the existing euro cells as Netherlands research hypotheses; do not copy €9.99/€14.99 into every country or treat the21%VAT calculator default as global. No Plus entitlement or billing is implemented.

- **Choose markets separately from language.** Google's [regional-pricing codelab](https://codelabs.developers.google.com/codelabs/play-billing-unlock-new-markets-regional-pricing) uses the customer's Play country to determine price currency. English copy therefore does not imply dollars. Future regional testing can use license testers and Play Billing Lab; no tester/account/catalog setup was performed here.
- **Configure the actual offer.** The current [one-time-product model](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/16430488?hl=en) separates product, purchase option and offer. Purchase options carry price and regional availability; app distribution still limits where buying is possible. For a possible permanent Plus unlock, evaluate a Buy option, not a rental. Owner confirmation of initial countries, product scope and account setup remains pending.
- **Treat conversion as a starting value.** Play documents one-off currency conversion across selected regions, with later price updates possible. This does not establish willingness to pay or local affordability. Keep research responses in participants' actual buying currencies and distinguish price conversion from a demand experiment. Do not invent regional price points from one competitor review.
- **Display the selected store offer.** A future paywall should use the queried eligible offer's [formatted price](https://developer.android.com/reference/com/android/billingclient/api/ProductDetails.OneTimePurchaseOfferDetails), with its currency, rather than an English-locale/USD guess or a hardcoded research price. The API explicitly excludes tax in tax-exclusive countries; never label every displayed amount an all-inclusive checkout total. Price lookup/offer unavailability must not disturb free protection.

Before any paid release, verify the active offer, returned price/currency, app/product region overlap and checkout behavior for each chosen market using the owner's actual catalog and current account/tax/fee terms. The codelab's example values and instructions to skip tax/compliance setup are tutorial shortcuts, not Ollo launch decisions. This is source-backed preparation only: no SDK, product, transaction, public price, fee assumption or external data flow changed.

## Small-sample validation plan

Run a first round with 8–12 Android users who have tried a blocker, including people who pay for apps and people who avoid subscriptions. This is directional discovery, not statistical evidence. Recruit without presenting the preferred price. Ask participants to try the current free Ollo flow first, then:

1. Ask which moment in the app they would return to, what felt valuable, and what they would remove. Observe the setup and a real intentional opening without coaching.
2. Ask what they currently use or pay for, with platform and purchase channel. Do not infer Android pricing from an iOS receipt.
3. Explain the proposed free core and the precise candidate Plus additions. Ask what they expect to remain free, what feels unfair to charge for, and what would make them distrust the app.
4. Before showing any price card, run the four open-ended Van Westendorp questions for the same one-time offer: at what price is it so cheap that quality is doubtful; what feels like a bargain; what feels expensive but still worth considering; and what is too expensive to consider? Capture one threshold answer to each question without suggesting price points.
5. Only after those open questions, assign each participant one monadic price card (€9.99 or €14.99) at random, balancing assignments across the sample. Show the same feature description, then ask whether they would buy at that price and why. Each participant sees only one price card, reducing anchoring and comparison effects. Record responses by assigned price, prior paid-app behavior, and reason for refusal. Do not treat stated intent as purchase conversion. Follow with a small Play internal-testing experiment using actual, clearly disclosed purchases only after billing is implemented and store setup is complete; use a valid randomized price experiment if available, and measure offer-view-to-purchase plus refunds/cancellations. Keep core free protection equal across cells.

**Value metric for product learning:** report the share of prompted openings where a user completes the deliberate step (for example, an explicit conscious-open action or leaves the distraction app) and the share who successfully complete their intended task without breaking essential access. These are descriptive on-device measures, not “time saved” claims. Ask participants whether the gate helped; do not optimize for more blocking time, quiz completion for its own sake, or reduced use of useful apps.

## Sources and scope notes

Official competitor sources and Play listing snapshots above were reviewed on 16 October 2026. Google Play’s official [service-fee schedule](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/112622?hl=en) and [15% tier enrollment instructions](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10632485?hl=en) were re-opened and the HTML table checked: the first-$1M cell has `colspan="3"`; the 15% scenario therefore means an enrolled account within its eligible first-$1M earnings, not the standard new-install rate. Standard other-transaction rates combine to 25% for new installs and 30% for existing installs; the program row combines to 20% and 25%. RevenueCat’s threshold and all-MTR charge are from its official [pricing](https://www.revenuecat.com/pricing) and [billing documentation](https://www.revenuecat.com/docs/welcome/set-up-revenuecat/account-management), which explicitly gives $2,600 MTR → $26. For AppBlock and Opal, only the Android listing / Android-specific help was used for platform claims. one sec’s vendor-published euro schedule is reported as vendor-wide evidence, with its exact Android Play price explicitly unverified. Store prices and fees can change by region and effective date. Before publishing actual prices, check the current Netherlands Google Play product catalog and account-specific fee enrollment. Ollo version 0.2.0-review has no billing, and no payments are currently being taken.

**Unresolved fee-base question:** Google’s [service-fee explanation](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/11131145?hl=en-en) says the fee is “based on a percentage of the purchase price” but does not establish whether that base is the Netherlands VAT-inclusive customer charge or the tax-exclusive item price. Google’s [tax guidance](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/138000?hl=en-en) says Netherlands buyer-facing prices include tax; its [invoice-report field definitions](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/6135870?hl=en) separately identify a tax-exclusive item price on user-charge lines and Google fee amounts on fee lines, without specifying the fee formula. The table therefore keeps its VAT-inclusive-gross fee basis as an unverified modeling assumption; its proceeds are not verified Play payouts. Resolve the base against current account-specific Play Console reporting before using these figures operationally.

**17 October source clarification:** Google's tax guidance does explicitly illustrate calculating the fee on the net product price where the developer remits VAT. Its EU section separately says Google remits VAT on EU purchases through Play Billing. That conditional example provides a documented tax-exclusive scenario; it does not, by itself, establish Ollo's Netherlands settlement basis. The local decision model therefore exposes both bases as assumptions. The fee schedule, fee explanation and tax guidance were reopened on 17 October; Ollo's enrollment, transaction classification and actual account reporting remain unverified.
