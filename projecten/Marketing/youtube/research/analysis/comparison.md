## What both samples consistently do

1. **Start with a concrete object.** A price, percentage, model size, button behavior or decisive claim appears in the first captioned sentence. Evidence includes [Devsplainers at 0:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=F3rmpMNoZP4&t=0s), [Kai oLLM at 0:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5E3qTmQPmP8&t=0s), and [Kai HTMX at 0:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HPAh1uiE2z0&t=0s).
2. **Delay broad context until the tension is legible.** Devsplainers explains attention only after the API bill; Kai explains the HTML mechanism only after the button behavior and no-JavaScript reveal.
3. **Build around a mechanism, test or comparison.** The narration supplies natural jobs for diagrams, code, UI and evidence labels.
4. **Use specific titles.** Named tools and constraints are more common than generic “AI news” labels.
5. **Speak quickly.** Caption estimates cover 141–172 WPM for Devsplainers and 165–192 WPM for Kai.

## What is useful for this channel

Adopt an original **proof-to-mechanism** format:

1. Show a verified output, failure or tradeoff.
2. State the exact promise within 15 seconds.
3. Explain only the mechanism needed to understand the result.
4. Demonstrate the tool, code or workflow.
5. Name limitations and uncontrolled variables.
6. End with a repeatable decision rule.

Primary positioning: **practical AI decisions proven with reproducible tasks**. This is narrower and more defensible than general AI news.

## What becomes copying or automation slop

- Recreating Devsplainers' dark/yellow diagram identity.
- Mirroring Kai's hype words, cancellation stories or thumbnail layouts.
- Rewriting either creator's script around the same examples.
- Generating dozens of videos from one template with swapped tool names.
- Using decorative motion that does not prove or explain narration.
- Claiming first-hand tests, cost savings, privacy or benchmark outcomes that were not verified.
